\subsection{Societal impact}
\secinfo{Jimena}{Grégoire}
The use of FHE requires a deep, specialized understanding of both cryptography and hardware architecture. By evaluating a compiler that decouples application logic from hardware accelerators, our project shows how developers can implement advanced encryption without the need to be cryptographers. Finally, sine standard cloud processing requires data to be decrypted in memory, which makes it vulnerable, the use of HEIR moves us (and the company) to a zero-trust model where even cloud providers or malicious insiders cannot access the plain text data during computation.
Currently, theirs a lot of public awareness of data harvesting and unauthorized data brokering has severely eroded thrust in single sign-on services, as users fear their personal networks are being exploited or sold. If Fully Homomorphic encryption becomes highly efficient through compilers like HEIR, it solves this trust deficit from the users. 

\subsection{Economic impact}
\secinfo{Jimena}{Grégoire}
FHE is really resource-intensive, so the systematic benchmarking of sorting and shuffling algorithms across CPUs will provide business with comprehensible metrics on running costs. Also, writing custom hardware-specific code optimizations is highly expensive, specially for encrypted operations. If HEIR allows developers to write code once and running it anywhere, it  decreases the cost required to deploy encrypted workloads and a reduction of software development time. 





\subsection{Research impact}
\secinfo{Abdul Moiz Akbar}{Louis Nathan}

The main research contribution of this project is measured data and a reusable benchmark suit for a type of workload that FHE compilers have not been evaluated on. HEIR's own examples focus almost entirely on machine learning and linear operations, and the repository contains no sorting or shuffling program \cite{Goo26}.So we show how well HEIR handles non-linear, data-oblivious programs, which existing evaluation do not cover.
\newline
\newline
A second contribution is the benchmark it self. Comparing FHE tools is difficult because there are no standardised benchmarks \cite{viand2021sok}, and earlier work has for benchmark suits to make libraries and compilers comparable \cite{gouert2022sok}. By comparing HEIR's output with a hand-written TFHE-rs implementation, we quantify how much performance is lost in exchange for writing a program once and compiling it for different backends.
\newline
\newline
Our results are also relevant to applications that build on encrypted
sorting. Sorting underlies database operations such as top-$k$ queries,
medians and ranking \cite{mazzone2024ranking}. Bugs and missing features we might encounter will be reported to the HEIR project , and our sorting examples could be contributed to Roseman Labs' confidentiality requirements. 
\newline
\newline
The research impact also has limitations. Our results are tied to one pinned
HEIR release, and since HEIR changes quickly \cite{Goo26}, absolute numbers
may be outdated within months. However, the reproducible suite is intended to offset
this. Furthermore, the small list sizes we can process limit how far our
findings generalise to large datasets.


\subsection{Other impact}
\secinfo{Abdul Moiz Akbar}{Louis Nathan}

Other than research, our work has an impact on energy use, data protection law, and long-term security. However, one of the most important negative impact is environmental. FHE has a huge energy cost, a 2025 study shows a 100,000 times higher energy use for encrypted machine learning than for the plaintext equivalent \cite{damie2025energy}. Our own experiments also consume
substantial compute, which we limit by testing on small inputs first and
stopping runs at a fixed time and memory budget.
\newline
\newline
Our work also has legal relevance: under the GDPR, encrypted data still
counts as personal data \cite{edpb2025pseudo}, so FHE lowers risk but does not
remove legal obligations. Our cost figures help organisations judge whether
FHE is a practical safeguard, and support the adoption of FHE.
Finally, the schemes targeted by HEIR are based on the Learning With Errors
problem, which is believed to resist quantum attacks \cite{regev2009lwe}.
Our documented benchmark suite also lowers the entry barrier for developers
who want to use FHE and transfers practical HEIR experience to Roseman Labs.
